SIG.01 // DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY

How I decide what good looks like

Before software I taught math — calculus at FAU, everything from arithmetic to differential equations at Mathnasium. That shaped how I build more than any framework has. These are the four rules I keep coming back to.

AXIOM_01

The test is the design

A failing test written first is a design decision made explicit. Instead of architecting a whole system in my head and hoping the output matches, I let the red–green–refactor loop force the interface into existence one honest claim at a time.

AXIOM_02

Teach it to learn it

Years of tutoring taught me that you don't understand a thing until you can explain it to someone who doesn't. I write, document, and name code for the person catching up — because six months from now, that person is me.

AXIOM_03

Small loops beat grand plans

Every project here started as the smallest thing that could work — a watchlist before a social network, one route before a REST API. Tight iterations surface the real problems early, while they're still cheap to fix.

AXIOM_04

Proof over promise

In math, a claim without a proof is just a conjecture. Same for software: "it works" means a passing suite, a live demo, and a measurable result — not a confident shrug. If I can't demonstrate it, I don't ship it.
